    How priorities are calculated

    The score uses 30 points for surface heat, 25 for lack of vegetation, and 15 each for lower income, the share of Black and mixed-race residents, and the share living on block faces without trees. Heat and income percentiles use the current scale: São Paulo districts or Brasilândia census areas. Compare scores within the same scale. The weights are a planning proposal for review with residents and partner organizations.

    The race factor is an area-level equity measure. An area needs all five indicators, at least five valid satellite pixels and at least 80% valid pixel coverage to receive a score. Income is the median for household heads with earnings.

    Urban forestry and climate justice

    More shade where São Paulo needs it most

    Ybirá is a hackathon prototype that helps residents, NGOs and public teams decide where to look for planting opportunities and which native trees could fit.

    Explore São Paulo

    Why shade is a climate justice issue

    On the walk to a bus stop or school, a street with few trees offers little shade. In São Paulo, exposure to heat overlaps with inequalities in income, race and access to green space. Ybirá brings those patterns into the same map so teams can see which areas warrant a closer look.

    Planting native trees can support local biodiversity as well as increase shade as the trees grow. The project connects that long-term goal to two decisions people can make today: where to visit first and what to consider planting when they get there.

    How the map, planting guide and AI work

    How areas are ranked

    The map combines Landsat, GeoSampa, the IBGE 2022 Census and vegetation data from Rede Nossa São Paulo. The score weights surface heat (30%), lack of vegetation (25%), lower household-head income (15%), the share of Black and mixed-race residents (15%) and residents on blocks without trees (15%).

    How trees are recommended

    When you describe a spot, AI fills in details like sidewalk width and overhead wires for you to check. Once you confirm the details, the guide applies city and prototype rules, then ranks the native trees that fit using the project catalog.

    The heat map shows land surface temperature from one satellite pass. Before planting, measure the spot and confirm local requirements and any needed permissions.

    Sources: GeoSampa (CC BY-SA), IBGE, Landsat/USGS, Rede Nossa São Paulo (district vegetation) and Municipal planting guide.
